# What I applied from short-form launch-video practice

I based these on a quick survey of short-form and launch-video guides (CapCut hook patterns, HubSpot short-form
trends, Flowjam and Vidico launch-video roundups, B2W app-promo examples). These are the choices that made it into this cut:

- **Hook in the first frame, not after an intro.** Frame 0 already shows the promise ("5 puzzles a day. 0 effort.")
  and all five boards, which solve themselves within about 2 s. The first frame works as a thumbnail, and there is no logo sting.
- **Show the product working early, and sell the outcome.** Real boards are solved by the real solvers by second 1.
  The brand name comes in only after the payoff ("Let LarpedIn play them for you").
- **Captions burned in for sound-off viewing.** Chunks of 1–3 words sit in the lower-middle safe zone, outside the platform UI
  at the bottom. The spoken word is highlighted. Game names are highlighted in each game's own colour, so the captions also
  teach the colour coding.
- **One idea per scene.** Hook, then Reads, Solves and Plays (one hero board, one big label, one supporting stat each),
  then "every daily game", with Wend in the hero slot, then the CTA. Every scene matches exactly one narration line.
- **Fast pacing locked to the beat.** Narration lines start on the 90 BPM beat grid of the music, and visual cuts land
  0.12 s ahead of each line. Transitions are 0.25 s fades or pops with no long dissolves. Tiles pop when the voice names them.
- **Short and vertical.** ~37 s at 1080×1920, within the 20–40 s sweet spot. Narration is 78 words at 1.05× speed.
- **A clear, low-friction CTA.** It asks a question ("Which puzzle should it beat next?") to drive comments,
  rather than a link we can't promise. A small "Not affiliated with LinkedIn" line sits under it.
- **Audio that stays out of the way.** A calm lo-fi bed is ducked under the voice, the whole mix is normalized to −14 LUFS
  (the common platform target), and it has a soft fade-out so the loop restarts cleanly.
